Women living with HIV face a substantially higher risk of cervical cancer

Women living with HIV need clear information and reliable access to HPV prevention, cervical screening and follow-up care.

Persistent infection with high-risk types of human papillomavirus (HPV) causes almost all cervical cancers. A weakened immune system can make it harder for the body to clear HPV, which increases the chance that abnormal cervical cells may develop and progress.

The World Health Organization reports that women living with HIV are about six times more likely to develop cervical cancer than women without HIV. This makes regular conversations with healthcare professionals especially important. Screening schedules should be based on national guidance and each woman's medical history.

What women can do

  • Ask a healthcare professional when cervical screening is recommended.
  • Attend follow-up appointments after an abnormal HPV or Pap test result.
  • Take HIV treatment as prescribed and remain engaged in routine care.
  • Seek medical advice about unusual vaginal bleeding, discharge, pelvic pain or pain during sex.
  • Ask whether HPV vaccination is appropriate and available.

Screening can identify high-risk HPV infection or abnormal cells before cervical cancer develops. An abnormal screening result does not necessarily mean cancer, but it should be followed up promptly.

Medical information notice

This article provides general information and does not replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
